Our Work Is Never Done,
an 800-page book on Mexican‑American
labor and protest, 1930s–1970s.

Project Description

Our Work Is Never Done, an 800‑page book on Mexican‑American immigration, features case studies of non‑violent protests, community injustices, and stories of labor from the 1930s–1970s, offering a powerful narrative of Mexican resilience and community.

Designed as an intimate read, the book uses a one‑column format, long image sequences, and personal stories. Its small trim size evokes a field journal, while its unusual length creates a bible‑like presence in the hand. Created in reaction to the current administration’s views and actions on immigration, the book is a passionate, content‑rich record of Mexican‑American history.

Styles Make Fights

As martial arts become increasingly politicized and reduced to ideas of national or cultural ownership, Styles Make Fights responds by showing combat sports as a global exchange of techniques, traditions, and cultures. Rather than focusing on one style or region, the book explores how these disciplines have evolved, influenced one another, and continue to shape modern combat sports.

By tracing these connections, the book aims to destigmatize combat sports and present them as an art form rooted in community. Through graphics, stories, and interviews, it explores the people, traditions, and shared influences that connect fighting styles across regions and continents, revealing combat sports as a global culture.

San Diego Football Club

This proposed rebrand of San Diego FC was developed for the club’s inaugural season, building a bold, cinematic identity rooted in the character and history of San Diego. The crest combines mission‑inspired arches, a shield‑shaped outline, and a custom anchor, all designed to symbolize the city’s architecture, proximity to the Pacific coastline, naval heritage, and military community.

The visual and promotional language is built around fan engagement and positive mantras and slogans such as “The City’s Anchor,” “Our Strength, Our Anchor,” and “Making Waves That Matter.” The rebrand embraces the energy of the team and its supporters, intentionally moving away from the increasingly corporate design and language of modern sports in favor of something bold, local, and community‑driven.

Project Description

This proposed rebrand of the World Rally Championship and its streaming service, Rally.TV, is designed to attract new viewers, building on the recent rise in Formula 1 viewership under the same governing body, the FIA.

A visual system inspired by important details of rally racing, referencing the physical forms that appear in racing, through the weight reduction concept and a typeface inspired by the shape language of track iconography, as well as the spirited, enthusiast side of motorsport, through posters and layouts inspired by vintage automotive advertisements.

Promotional materials are aimed at building interest in the sport and teaching fans more about it as they enjoy it, alongside user interface design that prioritizes live streaming.

Organico Bello

This proposed rebrand of Organico Bello centers the packaging around the quality and simplicity of its ingredients. Instead of following other brands that place the tomato front and center, the design leaves it off the label, allowing the product itself to stand out.

Made from recycled Birch paper that matches the natural color of fresh pasta, the packaging becomes a canvas for the ingredients themselves. The colors of the tomato‑based ingredients visually coat the pasta‑colored packaging, creating a direct connection between the food and its container. Cutouts keep the food visible, while the glass jars preserve the ingredients while, most importantly, keeping them visible from every angle. The custom label wraps frame the product rather than conceal it like a standard label.

Rooted in Naples’ tradition of wholesome, ingredient‑first cooking, the packaging follows the same philosophy as the food itself: let the ingredients speak for themselves.
